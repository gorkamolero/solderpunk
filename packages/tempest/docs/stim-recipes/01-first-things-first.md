## Introduction

This article introduces foundational drum synthesis principles for the DSI Tempest synthesizer. The author emphasizes that the Tempest requires programming rather than preset selection, and recommends using visualization tools like spectrum analyzers while learning sound design.

## Key Concepts

### Envelopes

Envelopes shape sound parameters over time. The Tempest provides five envelopes per sound, with Amplitude and Cutoff hardwired and three additional (Pitch/Aux1/Aux2) mappable to specific parameters.

Drums follow an Attack-Decay-Release pattern: "A drum is invariably hit with something...There is thus an instant Attack and a short Decay...before the Release" consisting of residual vibrations.

The Tempest's unique "Peak" parameter holds sound at maximum before decay, providing additional control over transients.

### Velocity Mapping

"The most important expression tool on a drumsynth," velocity mapping creates human-like variations between hits. The ModPaths menu allows mapping velocity to amplitude, filter, and pitch among other targets.

### Oscillators

The Tempest features two analog and two digital oscillators per voice. Analog oscillators run free by default but require "Wave reset" enabled for consistent drum sounds. Setting "Key follow" to Off maintains pitch consistency across hits.

### Filters

Filters remove extreme frequencies: "A combination of Lowpass and Highpass filters = Bandpass filter. This gives you decent control over the frequencies taken-up by each sound."

The lowpass can switch between 4-pole and 2-pole slopes for smoother effects.

## Practical Considerations

The Tempest's envelopes are notably fast, producing clicks at default settings. Slightly increasing Attack smooths this effect. Tuning drums to fit song keys improves overall kit cohesion and clarity.
